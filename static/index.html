Why this sometimes refuses to answer

An HS code on a customs entry is a legal declaration. Filing the wrong one is a false statement under 19 U.S.C. §1592, and the penalty follows the importer of record — not the tool that suggested the code.

A classifier that always returns something is optimising to look useful. This one withholds the answer whenever the retrieved rulings don't support it, tells you that is what happened, and still shows you every ruling it consulted so you are further along than when you started.

Questions

Where do the answers come from?

U.S. Customs and Border Protection publishes its classification rulings through CROSS — decades of decisions on specific goods, each naming the subheading CBP assigned and why. Every answer here is drawn from that corpus, and every ruling used is linked.

What is a 6-digit HS code?

The first six digits are the international Harmonized System subheading, identical across every member country. The digits after that are national — the US adds four more for the full 10-digit HTS line that goes on an entry.

Is this customs advice?

No, and it is not a binding ruling. Only CBP can issue one, and you can request it free through the same system these answers come from. For a shipment that matters, have a licensed customs broker confirm the classification.

Is there an API?

Yes — the same classification and the same gate, as JSON. It is not open for signups yet.
