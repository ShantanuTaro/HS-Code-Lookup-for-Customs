    How it works

    01

    Describe what you're importing

    Write in plain English. Include material, function, and whether the article is finished or unfinished. More detail increases retrieval precision.

    02

    Rulings are retrieved and graded

    We query {{COUNT}} indexed CBP rulings and rank the ones that match your description. A scoring model measures confidence across the result set.

    03

    You get the code or an honest refusal

    If confidence is sufficient and the leading rulings agree, you get the 6-digit HS code and every ruling behind it. If not, we say so.

    Why this sometimes refuses

    A wrong HS code is a false statement to CBP.

    Under 19 U.S.C. §1592, submitting an incorrect tariff classification - even negligently - can result in civil penalties up to the full value of the merchandise. This tool refuses to classify when the indexed CBP rulings don't clearly support an answer.

    A refusal is not a failure. It tells you the question needs more work: a binding ruling request directly to CBP, or a consultation with a licensed customs broker.

    FAQ

    Is this tool free?

    Yes. No account, no subscription, no fee. CBP rulings are public records - this indexes and queries them.

    What are CBP rulings?

    U.S. Customs and Border Protection publishes its tariff classification rulings through the CROSS database. Each one records how a specific described product was classified under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule, and why.

    What is a 6-digit HS code?

    The first six digits are the international Harmonized System subheading, identical across every member country. The digits after that are national - the US adds four more for the full 10-digit HTS line that goes on an entry.

    Why does the tool sometimes refuse to answer?

    When no CBP ruling closely matches your description, or when the relevant rulings conflict, the classification is withheld. Filing a wrong HS code is a false statement to CBP under 19 U.S.C. §1592. It refuses rather than guesses.

    Can I rely on this for an actual shipment?

    This is for research and orientation. For a binding determination, request a ruling from CBP through CROSS - it is free - or consult a licensed customs broker. Only a CBP-issued binding ruling protects you from reclassification at the border.
